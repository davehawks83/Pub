Family Trail Guide • Glen Arbor, Michigan

Sleeping Bear Dunes with a 1- & 3-year-old

Trip window: Oct. 6–10, 2026. Prioritized for short, high-payoff hikes, carrier practicality, preschooler stamina, fall scenery and a morning-adventure → lunch → nap rhythm.

Best four: Empire Bluff, Pyramid Point, Windy Moraine and Cottonwood. Good Harbor Bay is the best easier / lower-stress backup.

#1

Empire Bluff Trail

Best overall hike for this trip: big Lake Michigan payoff without a long mileage commitment.

🥾 1.5 mi round trip🚗 ~15–20 min📈 Moderate🛒 Stroller: No🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 10/10

Terrain

Beech-maple forest, rolling hills, bluff-side boardwalk and overlook.

Realistic time

Allow about 1¼–2 hours with a 1- and 3-year-old.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

The 3-year-old can likely walk a substantial portion, but expect some carrying on the hills. Use a carrier for the 1-year-old.

Safety / practical note

The bluff is very steep. Keep the 3-year-old close/hand-held near the overlook and do not descend the bluff.

Best pairing: Empire Beach, the playground and lunch in Empire before heading home for nap.

#2

Pyramid Point

Best huge-view-to-mileage ratio. Do the overlook out-and-back rather than the full 2.7-mile loop.

🥾 1.2 mi round trip to overlook🚗 ~15–20 min📈 Moderate🛒 Stroller: No🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 9.5/10

Terrain

Old beech-maple forest, rolling grades and a dramatic high bluff above Lake Michigan.

Realistic time

About 1–1½ hours at toddler pace.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

The 0.6-mile walk to the overlook is a manageable goal for a preschooler. Carrier for the 1-year-old.

Safety / practical note

Another steep bluff. Stay on the established overlook and do not descend.

Best pairing: A slow drive through Port Oneida Rural Historic District to see old farms, barns and fall color.

#3

Windy Moraine Trail

The sleeper pick for an October trip: forest, old farm fields, glacial landscape and Glen Lake views.

🥾 1.5 mi loop🚗 ~10–15 min📈 Easy–moderate🛒 Stroller: No🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 9/10 in October

Terrain

Meadow, rolling moraine hills, beech-maple forest and a somewhat quicker descent near the end.

Realistic time

About 1–1½ hours.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

A good candidate for the 3-year-old to walk most of the trail because footing is easier than deep dune sand.

Safety / practical note

Normal woodland-hike precautions; watch roots and leaf-covered uneven ground.

Best pairing: A short stop at the Dune Climb so the kids can play in the sand before lunch.

#4

Cottonwood Trail

The best short hike for actually being out in the high dune landscape rather than only viewing it.

🥾 1.5 mi loop🚗 ~15–20 min + Pierce Stocking📈 Moderate / sandy🛒 Stroller: No🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 9/10

Terrain

Exposed sand, dune grasses, shrubs and scattered cottonwoods on the Sleeping Bear Plateau.

Realistic time

NPS suggests about an hour; with little kids, budget 1–1½ hours.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

Short mileage but sand makes it harder. The 3-year-old may love the giant-sandbox feel; use a carrier for the 1-year-old.

Safety / practical note

Wind and sun exposure can be stronger on the plateau. Bring water and layers.

Best pairing: Make it part of a big day on Pierce Stocking Scenic Drive with overlooks, the Dune Climb and Glen Haven.

#5

Good Harbor Bay Trail

Best lower-stress hiking day: mostly flat and varied, with dunes, woods, wetlands and nearby beach access.

🥾 Up to 2.8 mi loop🚗 ~20–25 min📈 Easy🛒 Stroller: Not ideal🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 8.5/10

Terrain

Low coastal dunes, sandy path, forest and wetland with a small bridge.

Realistic time

About 1½–2 hours if you complete the full loop.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

The flatter profile gives the 3-year-old the best chance of walking a lot of it. Turning around early is completely reasonable.

Safety / practical note

Wet areas can be muddy; check footing on bridges and damp sections.

Best pairing: Good Harbor Beach for snacks, rock hunting and shoreline exploration.

#6

Bay View Trail — Farm Loop

Best historic farm-country walk: fields, old farms, forest edges and broad northern-Michigan scenery.

🥾 2.4 mi🚗 ~15–20 min📈 Easy–moderate🛒 Stroller: No on hiking loop🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 8/10

Terrain

Fields, farm lanes, wooded sections and some rolling hills.

Realistic time

Plan about 1½–2 hours with stops.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

Less instant payoff than the bluff trails, but lots of room to explore and excellent fall atmosphere.

Safety / practical note

Open areas can feel chilly/windy in October; bring a layer.

Best pairing: If everyone wants an easier outing, substitute a section of the stroller-friendly Sleeping Bear Heritage Trail nearby.

#7

Alligator Hill — Easy Route

A convenient forest option close to Glen Arbor, with views toward Lake Michigan, the Manitou Islands and Glen Lake depending on route.

🥾 Up to 3 mi loop🚗 ~10 min📈 Moderate for toddlers🛒 Stroller: No🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 7.5/10

Terrain

Wide, well-marked old road beds through hilly maple-beech forest.

Realistic time

Allow 1½–2+ hours if doing the 3-mile loop.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

The 3-mile loop is longer than your best options. A simple out-and-back toward the lookout is often smarter with little kids.

Safety / practical note

Long gradual grades add up when carrying a child. Turn around before everyone is tired.

Best pairing: Use this as the 'we don't want to drive anywhere' morning: hike, lunch in Glen Arbor, home for nap.

#8

Sleeping Bear Point Trail

Spectacular dunes and Lake Michigan, but deep sand makes this much harder than the mileage suggests.

🥾 Partial hike or 2.8 mi loop🚗 ~10 min📈 Strenuous🛒 Stroller: No🎒 Carrier: Yes⭐ 7/10 for this age

Terrain

Rolling sand dunes, open exposure, forest pockets and Lake Michigan access.

Realistic time

Potentially 2+ hours for the full loop at family pace.

With a 1- & 3-year-old

A partial out-and-back is the better choice. The risk is a preschooler tiring after a long sandy stretch while you're already carrying the 1-year-old.

Safety / practical note

Bring water, shoes, sun/wind protection and stay on marked posts in the dunes.

Best pairing: If you want a true dune hike with less commitment, Cottonwood is the better fit for this trip.

Official trail information ↗

Bonus: Sleeping Bear Heritage Trail

Your stroller-day secret weapon. It is a hard-surfaced multi-use trail through the Lakeshore, and NPS identifies it as suitable for people pushing baby strollers. Pick a short section near Glen Haven, the Dune Climb or Alligator Hill and turn around when it stops being fun.

Best use: baby needs a stroller nap + preschooler wants to walk + adults still want to be outdoors.

October practical notes

Layers: Lake Michigan wind can make bluff overlooks feel much colder. Bring hats and an extra layer.

Leaf-covered trails: Roots and uneven ground are harder to see. A carrier is safer than forcing a stroller onto dirt trails.

Bluffs: Empire Bluff and Pyramid Point require close supervision. The drop-offs are real.

Hunting season: Hunting is permitted in portions of the Lakeshore. Bright outer layers are sensible on quiet wooded trails in October.

Toddler rule: A 45-minute out-and-back everyone enjoys is better than finishing a loop with two exhausted children.
